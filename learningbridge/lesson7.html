Personal Information

Latin American Edition · Culture-based language learning

Learning Goal

I can give basic personal information on a simple form.

Grammar Formula

What + be + your + noun?
What is your name?
What is your country?
What is your city?
My + noun + is + information.
My country is Ecuador.

Key Vocabulary
personal information form ID card name country city nationality date of birth student information
Grammar in Context

Use What is your...? to ask for personal information.
Use My... is... to answer.
Use short, clear answers on simple forms.

Before Reading

What information do schools usually ask for on a student form?

Culture Reading

The Cédula and Personal Information

In many Latin American countries, people use an identification card for important activities. In some places, this card is called a cédula. It can include basic information such as complete name, nationality, date of birth, and identification number. This information helps schools, offices, banks, and public services identify a person.

Personal information is also common on school forms. A simple student form can ask for first name, last name, country, city, age, and contact information. These details are basic, but they must be clear and correct. A small spelling mistake can change important information.

In English, students can learn to ask and answer simple questions about personal information. For example: “What is your name?” “What is your country?” “What is your city?” The answers can be short: “My name is Ana.” “My country is Ecuador.” “My city is Quito.”

Personal information must also be safe. Students should practice with sample forms in class, but they should not share private information with strangers. Learning personal information in English helps students complete forms, introduce themselves, and communicate in school or travel situations.
