Numbers Around Us

Latin American Edition · Culture-based language learning

Learning Goal

I can use numbers in everyday places.

Grammar Formula

Number + noun
one kilo
two bags
three apples
How many + plural noun?
How many bananas?
There are + number + plural noun.
There are five oranges.

Key Vocabulary
number price market fruit vegetable kilo bag coins sign quantity
Grammar in Context

Use numbers before nouns.
Use How many...? to ask about quantity.
Use There are with plural nouns.

Before Reading

Where do you see numbers every day? Think about markets, buses, prices, houses, and phones.

Culture Reading

Numbers in a Latin American Market

Numbers are everywhere in daily life. In a Latin American market, people can see numbers on signs, bags, boxes, and price lists. A market is a colorful place with fruit, vegetables, grains, flowers, and many local products. Numbers help buyers and sellers communicate clearly.

A sign can say: two kilos of potatoes, three bananas, five oranges, or one bag of rice. A price can be written on a small card. People use numbers to ask for quantities, check prices, and pay with coins or bills. Even a simple number can be very important in a busy market.

Markets are also cultural spaces. In Ecuador, Colombia, Peru, Mexico, and many other countries, markets connect families, farmers, and communities. People can buy food, talk with sellers, and learn about local products. The language is simple but useful: "How many?" "How much?" "Two kilos, please."

Learning numbers in English helps students talk about real objects around them. Numbers are useful in class, at home, in stores, on streets, and online. They are small words with big value.

Reading Check

  3. Why are numbers important for buyers and sellers?
  4. What communities are connected by markets?
  5. Where else are numbers useful?
