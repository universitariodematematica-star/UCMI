What Time Is It?

Latin American Edition · Culture-based language learning

Learning Goal

I can ask and answer about the time.

Grammar Formula

What time + be + it?
What time is it?
It is seven o’clock.
It is half past eight.
at + time
School starts at seven o’clock.

Key Vocabulary
timel clock hour minute o'clock half past morning afternoon school day schedule
Grammar in Context

Use What time is it? to ask about the time.
Use It is... to answer.
Use at + time to say when an activity happens.

Before Reading

What time does your school day start?

What time do you eat lunch?

Time in a School Day

Time helps students organize a school day. In many Latin American schools, students follow a schedule with start time, class time, break time, lunch time, and end time. A clock can be in a classroom, office, hallway, or schoolyard. Students and teachers use time to move from one activity to another.

A school day can start early in the morning. Some students begin classes at seven o’clock. Others start at eight o’clock. A class can last forty or fifty minutes. There can be a break in the morning, and students can eat, play, talk, or rest for a short time.

Time is also important outside school. Students use time for homework, family activities, sports, music practice, and rest. In cities, time can be connected with buses and transportation. In rural areas, time can be connected with walking to school, family work, or community activities.

In English, students can ask: “What time is it?” They can answer: “It is seven o’clock” or “It is half past eight.” Learning time helps students describe real daily life.
