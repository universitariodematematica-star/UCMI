My Name and Last Name

Latin American Edition · Culture-based language learning

Learning Goal

I can say and spell my first name and last name.

Grammar Formula

My + noun + is + information
My first name is Andrés.
My last name is Pérez.
My family name is important.
What + be + your + noun?
What is your name?

Key Vocabulary
first name last name family name spell letter identity tradition father mother Latin America
Grammar in Context

Use My name is to give your complete name.
Use My first name is for your given name.
Use My last name is for your family name.
Use What is your...? to ask for information.

Before Reading

How many last names do people often use in your country? Why are names important?

Culture Reading

Family Names in Latin America

Names are very important in Latin America. A name is not only a word. It can show family, history, language, and identity. Many people have a first name and one or two last names. In many families, last names connect a person with parents, grandparents, and family stories.

In several Latin American countries, people commonly use two family names. One family name can come from the father's family, and another family name can come from the mother's family. This custom helps people remember both sides of the family. It also shows that identity is connected with more than one person.

First names can also have meaning. Some names come from relatives. Some names come from saints, historical people, nature, or family traditions. A person's name can be short, long, common, or unique. In English class, learning to say and spell your name is very useful.

When students introduce themselves, they can say: "My first name is..." and "My last name is..." They can also spell their names letter by letter. This is simple, but it is important for forms, schools, travel, and communication.
