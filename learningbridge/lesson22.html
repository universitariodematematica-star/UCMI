Furniture and Objects

Latin American Edition · Culture-based language learning

Learning Goal

I can name furniture and common objects at home.

Grammar Formula

This + is + a/an + object
This is a table.
This is a chair.
This is a blanket.
These + are + plural objects
These are chairs.

Key Vocabulary
furniture object table chair bed sofa door window blanket handmade
Grammar in Context

Use this is for one object.
Use these are for more than one object.
Use a/an before singular objects.

Before Reading

What handmade objects or furniture do people use in homes in your community?


Furniture and Handmade Objects

Furniture and objects make a home useful and comfortable. In many Latin American homes, people use tables, chairs, beds, sofas, shelves, doors, windows, blankets, baskets, and many other everyday objects. These objects help families eat, sleep, study, work, and rest.

Some objects in a home are handmade. In many communities, artisans make wooden tables, woven baskets, clay pots, blankets, textiles, hammocks, or decorative objects. Handmade objects can show local materials, family traditions, and cultural identity. A blanket, a basket, or a wooden chair can have a story behind it.

Furniture can also show the daily life of a family. A table can be a place for meals, homework, and conversation. A chair can be for visitors. A bed is for rest. A shelf can hold books, photos, or important objects. Simple objects can be very meaningful.

In English, students can identify home objects with short sentences: “This is a table.” “This is a blanket.” “These are chairs.” Learning these words helps students describe their home and the things around them.
