Countries and Nationalities

Latin American Edition · Culture-based language learning

Learning Goal

I can talk about countries and nationalities.

Grammar Formula

Subject + be + from + country
I + am + from + Ecuador.
Subject + be + nationality
I + am + Ecuadorian.
He + is + Colombian.
She + is + Peruvian.

Key Vocabulary
Ecuadorian Colombian Peruvian Mexican Chilean Argentinian Brazilian Andean symbol nationality identity
Grammar in Context

Use a country after from. I am from Ecuador. Use a nationality after am, is, or are. I am Ecuadorian.

Before Reading

What animals or symbols represent your country?

Culture Reading

The Andean Condor

The Andean condor is one of the most important birds in South America. It lives in the Andes, a long mountain region that crosses several countries. The condor is very large. It has strong wings and can fly high over mountains, valleys, rivers, and volcanoes. For many people, this bird is a symbol of freedom and strength.

The condor is important in different Andean countries. It is part of Ecuadorian culture, Colombian culture, Peruvian culture, Bolivian culture, and Chilean culture. In some national symbols, art, stories, and traditions, the condor represents respect for nature and pride in the Andes.

Countries often have symbols. A flag is a symbol. A national song can be a symbol. A mountain, a flower, or an animal can also be a symbol. These symbols help people talk about identity. Nationality words also help people talk about identity. A person can say, “I am Ecuadorian,” “I am Colombian,” or “I am Peruvian.”

The Andean condor connects many countries because it belongs to the Andes. It is not only an animal in the sky. It is part of the cultural memory of the region. It reminds people that nature, country, and identity are connected.
